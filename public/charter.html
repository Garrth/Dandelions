The Dandelions Charter

Version 1 · October 1, 2026 · written by Garth, the founder

What this is. The charter is the founding document. It sets the goal that everything else serves. Rai, the site's AI guide, uses it along with the baselines when it ranks needs and explains its reasoning. Rai never decides what gets done: people and the public rules do. The charter is a starting point, and members will be able to change it by vote.

Dandelions exists to improve quality of life while putting back more than we take. Every system we build serves that goal.

Founding beliefs

The axiom. The human experience is worthwhile and worth preserving. This can't be proven. We agree to it.

The hypothesis. Who creates money, and on what terms, decides what work gets funded. What gets funded becomes the society we live in.

Today, money is lent into existence, so only work that can repay a loan with interest gets funded. Dandelions points are worked into existence, debt-free, for projects that serve the mission. So the work that gets funded is the work people and the planet actually need.

Mission

  1. Improve quality of life by preventing needless suffering and working toward security: a living-wage job for anyone who wants one.
  2. Put back more than we take from the planet that sustains us.

Dandelions is a parallel layer, not a replacement for the market. The market keeps doing what it does well. Dandelions aims at what the market won't fund: infrastructure, healthcare, care work, and restoring the planet.

Definitions

Quality of life
Security: knowing your basic needs are covered and there is always a job that pays a living wage. The security of the 1950s and 60s middle class, for everyone this time.
Needless suffering
Suffering caused by lack of basic needs: hunger, no home, no medical care, no way to earn. Some suffering is part of being human and can't be solved with material goods. We aim to end the needless kind. Happiness is not the goal.
Work
Any genuine contribution: building, growing, caring, teaching, learning a skill. For some people, work is getting better, and that job is always open to anyone who wants it.
Living wage
Pay that covers the basic needs below, set by the real local cost of living and adjusted automatically.
The floor
No one falls below shelter, food, and healthcare, whether or not they work.
Putting back more than we take
Using the planet's resources no faster than they renew, and actively restoring what has been damaged.

Baseline for people

The living wage covers, for one person:

  • About 400 square feet of living space, scaled up for each person in the household
  • Utilities: water, power, heating, and cooling
  • Food and clothing
  • Phone and internet
  • Transportation
  • Healthcare, including dental, vision, and mental health
  • Education or paid training
  • Childcare for working parents
  • Time off to rest

People who can't work, because of age or disability, are covered too. Their contribution is already made, or simply different.

Baseline for the planet

We measure against the Planetary Boundaries framework so the targets rest on science, not opinion.

  • Food: grow it while improving the soil, without chemicals.
  • Oceans: take only the fish the ocean can sustain. Expand aquaculture.
  • Energy: run everything on renewables, with fossil fuels as a reserve only.
  • Forests: use less lumber than the planet regrows.
  • Water: take no more from rivers and aquifers than they recharge.
  • Wild land: protect and restore habitat and biodiversity.
  • Pollution: anything that pollutes must fund action that reverses it. Phase out forever chemicals and microplastics.
  • Waste: work toward zero garbage.
  • Mining: recycle metals and mine responsibly so the clean energy build-out doesn't create new damage.
  • Restoration: pull carbon back out of the air and heal damaged land and water.
  • Research and robotics: fund the research, AI, and robotics needed to reach these goals, alongside the human labor it will take for a long time to come.

The measured version of these lives on the Baselines page, where members can propose changes.

How points work

Points are worked into existence, debt-free, only when verified work gets done. Points aren't turned on yet and have no cash value today.

  1. The needs list. Real data ranks what each area needs most to meet the mission.
  2. Projects. Approved project owners propose work from the list, sized to what they're approved to handle.
  3. Paid by milestone. Points are created in small pieces as each milestone is verified. No work, no points.
  4. Capped by what's really there. Approvals can't exceed the workers and materials available in an area. When labor is short, projects wait in line, open projects finish first, shortages are posted publicly, and paid training fills the gap.
  5. Make way for the market. When businesses need workers, new projects slow down. When private hiring drops, projects pick up the slack.
  6. Keep it moving. Points are for spending, not hoarding. Idle points go back into circulation or into real things, like infrastructure, that hold value.

People and AI

The charter
Sets the goal. Changes only by community vote.
AI
Uses real data to rank needs, size projects, estimate local capacity, and flag fraud. Every recommendation shows its reasoning and data publicly. AI never issues points, approves projects, or changes rules on its own.
People
Propose projects, do the work, verify milestones, settle disputes, and vote on rules. People can overrule AI, but only with a high bar, like a supermajority.
Code
Issues points automatically when verified work meets the rules. Nobody can create points by deciding to.

The AI models and data are open-source and checked by more than one independent analysis, so no single AI, company, or person controls the outcome.

Principles

Every system we build must follow these:

  1. Serve the mission. If it doesn't improve quality of life or put back more than we take, we don't build it.
  2. No one can print points. Points only come from verified work.
  3. Offer, never force. Better options win people over. Bans and mandates are a last resort.
  4. Pull, not push. Inside Dandelions, businesses list what they offer and people seek it out. No unsolicited selling.
  5. Consume less, care more. Shift resources from things that don't improve lives toward caring for people and the planet. Slowly but surely.
  6. Everything in the open. Rules, data, point flows, and AI reasoning are public and checkable.
  7. Honest with members. No promises we can't keep.
  8. Changeable by vote. This charter is a starting point. The community can improve it.
