Under the hood

This is the short version of the plan Dandelions is testing. None of it is running yet. Right now Dandelions only collects pledges, so we can see how many people agree. There are no tokens and nothing to buy.

Money is a tool

What actually gets work done is people and materials. Money is how we measure and move them, the way a ruler measures lumber. When the people and the materials are both there and the work still doesn't happen, the tool is the problem.

Dandy: Picture a crew next to a pile of lumber, ready to go. And the boss says, "Sorry folks, we're out of inches."

Why the work waits

Most money today starts out as a loan, and a loan has to be paid back with interest. So the work that gets done first is the work that pays back fast.

Roads, clean water, healthy soil, and caring for people pay back slowly, or not in cash at all. So they wait. The market is great at a lot of things, but it isn't built for this. Government runs on taxes and borrowing, which run short right when jobs disappear.

What Dandelions tries instead

Pay for verified work, not loans. In short:

  1. A public needs list. Real data ranks what each area needs most.
  2. Local projects. Approved project owners take on work from the list, sized to what they can handle.
  3. Paid by milestone. Pay is created only when people check and confirm a milestone is done. No work, no pay.
  4. Capped by what's really there. Projects can't outgrow the local workers and materials.
  5. Make way for business. When businesses need workers, new projects slow down. When hiring drops, projects pick up the slack.

Andy: So the money only shows up when the work does?

Dandy: That's the whole trick. Nobody gets to print it just by deciding to.

Who decides what

  • AI recommends what to work on first, and shows its reasoning and data in public. It never pays anyone, approves projects, or changes the rules.
  • People propose projects, do the work, check each other's work, and vote on the rules. They can overrule the AI, but only with a big majority.
  • Code pays for verified work automatically, following rules everyone can read.

No one in charge

Dandelions doesn't depend on one leader. It starts with open-source code anyone can read. Then the data becomes public and checkable, then the records move to a shared public ledger, and finally the members run it by vote. Everything happens in the open: rules, data, and where the money goes.

How it starts

  1. Now: pledges. We count who agrees, county by county, on the map.
  2. Sprout, at 1% of a county. Phase 2 starts there: small local projects you can choose to join. The details get published when the first county gets there.
  3. After that: more counties and bigger projects, one step at a time, each one in the open.

There's no timeline. It grows as fast as people take part.

What we don't know yet

Plenty. Which projects go first, how pay works day to day, and the legal side. We'll get legal advice before anything like real tokens exists. We'd rather tell you that than pretend.
